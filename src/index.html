Dirty Dog

Bid it. Make it. Exactly.

Dealing in 7… (scroll the rules to stay here)

How to play

  • 3 to 6 players, one standard 52-card deck.
  • 15 hands. Deal 8 cards each, then 7, 6… down to a single card, then climb back up to 8.
  • The deal passes to the left each hand. Turn the next card face up: its suit is trump for that hand.
  • Starting left of the dealer, everyone bids how many tricks they'll take: the exact number, not “at least.”
  • Left of the dealer leads. Follow suit if you can. Highest trump wins the trick, otherwise the highest card of the suit led. The winner leads next.

Scoring

Make your bid exactly10 + tricks takenBid 3, took 3 → 13
Miss it, over or undertricks taken − 10Bid 3, took 1 → −9

Bidding zero and taking zero is worth a tidy 10. Highest total after the last 8-card hand wins.

Common house rule: the dealer bids last and may not make the bids add up to the number of cards, so somebody has to miss. The score sheet shows the bid total so you can enforce it if your table does.

A little history

Dirty Dog belongs to the Oh Hell family of exact-bidding trick-taking games, a branch of whist known around the world as Oh Pshaw, Blackout, Up and Down the River, Bust and Nomination Whist, among many other names, depending on who taught whom.

The family's whole hook is the exact bid: taking one trick too many hurts as much as one too few. Classic Oh Hell usually climbs from 1 card to 10 and back down. Dirty Dog flips it, opening big at 8 cards, shrinking to a nerve-wracking one-card hand in the middle, then building back up to 8.

Every table plays it a little differently. Your house rules win.
